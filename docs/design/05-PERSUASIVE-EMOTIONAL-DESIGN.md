# 05 — Persuasive and Emotional Design

## 1. Intended change

FOG OF SEA is persuasive in an educational sense: it encourages the player to slow down, make assumptions visible, connect means to political purpose, anticipate adaptation, and treat uncertainty as something to manage rather than hide.

It must not persuade through compulsion, surveillance, fabricated urgency, hidden scoring, or shame.

## 2. Emotional thesis

The experience follows a **cozy-sublime learning arc**:

```text
trust -> curiosity -> awe -> orientation -> analytical tension
      -> commitment -> uncertainty -> consequence -> repair -> renewed agency
```

- Cozy qualities—pastel color, glass, breathing motion, low-note ambiance, orderly text—reduce the intimidation of a dense strategy system.
- Sublime qualities—immense stars, weather, ocean, fog, aurora, and depth—make uncertainty felt.
- Rigid rules and explicit thresholds prevent beauty from becoming vagueness.
- Diagnostic repair prevents consequence from becoming humiliation.

## 3. Desired emotions by phase

| Phase | Primary emotion | Secondary emotion | Design means | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy | Trust | Control | Plain disclosure, equal session/save choices | Coercive consent or buried storage detail |
| Mission | Curiosity | Concern | Fictional brief, civilian context, dynamic world | Lore dump or triumphal framing |
| Strategy | Deliberation | Intellectual ownership | Ordered questions, optional prose | Correct-answer theater |
| Force | Competence | Productive friction | Compatibility, tradeoffs, visible credit | Shopping-list accumulation |
| Review | Readiness | Constructive doubt | Gaps, substitutions, environment fit | Surprise gate or opaque rejection |
| Command | Tension | Agency under uncertainty | Precommitment, ranges, reversible last turn | Casino-like rerolling or flashing alarm |
| Debrief | Consequence | Hopeful repair | Cause → Evidence → Adjustment | Moral judgment or score-only verdict |
| Academy | Curiosity | Mastery | Paths, checks, contrasts, notebook | Credential pretense or completion pressure |

## 4. Persuasive pattern inventory

| Pattern | Intended behavior | Mechanism | Ethical safeguard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Privacy-before-play | Make an informed storage choice | Timely disclosure | Session-only is fully functional and equally available |
| Ordered strategy steps | Frame purpose before force | Sequencing and commitment | Completed choices remain editable |
| Locked points | Prevent premature optimization | Constraint at the moment of temptation | Rule and unlock condition are visible |
| Compatibility gating | Learn systems relationships | Immediate causal feedback | Block only invalid action; suggest repair |
| Environment-fit review | Compare plan to conditions | Reflection before commitment | No hidden penalty is introduced here |
| Precommitted matrix | Accept uncertainty without chasing luck | Procedural trust | Ranges and no-reroll rule are disclosed |
| Exact undo | Experiment and learn | Safe reversibility | Same orders preserve same outcome |
| Cause/evidence/adjustment | Turn failure into learning | Attribution and feasible next step | Findings cite actual turn evidence |
| Related Academy lesson | Deepen understanding at need | Contextual learning | Optional, no interruption, no score effect |
| Session-only default path | Preserve autonomy | Data minimization | Portable TXT remains available |

## 5. Ethical persuasion principles

### 5.1 Autonomy

- No account or mandatory persistence.
- No streak, daily reward, countdown, loot, or variable-reward loop.
- No hidden penalty for opening help, undoing, or using Guided mode.
- No artificial scarcity or “last chance” copy.

### 5.2 Competence

- Explain rules at the point of consequence.
- Distinguish selected, compatible, hosted, credited, and effective.
- Give substitutions and adjustments, not merely warnings.
- Make optional prose unscored so reflection is not optimized for a rubric.

### 5.3 Relatedness and moral imagination

Mission briefs include civilian, neutral, infrastructure, evidence-custody, and transition concerns. These are decision constraints and protected interests, not background decoration.

### 5.4 Accuracy and epistemic humility

- Every scenario and capability is fictional.
- Scores are internal rule compliance, not forecasts.
- A single play is not a real-world probability.
- Concealed information is not replaced by suggestive animation or copy.
- Difficulty changes pressure, not the player’s worth.

## 6. Emotional use of graphics

### Stars

Dense crystalline lights create scale and wonder. No individual star becomes a badge or reward. Their bounded wandering and halos signal a living world; foreground occlusion maintains tactical readability and trust. Retaining a small brightest-light cohort through Dawn and Dusk sustains continuity across time presentations without turning twilight into night or changing the star material.

### Weather

Fog, rain, snow, cloud mass, and waves make friction embodied. Severity is continuous and readable. Weather cannot become a screen-wide overlay that erases agency, and it cannot appear in physically contradictory layers.

### Aurora

Aurora is rare enough to feel special but never used as an achievement reward. Its darkness-linked luminance and atmospheric eligibility preserve world coherence.

### Subject emission

The faint native-color breath helps vessels and aircraft remain enjoyable to observe in difficult light. It reads as dreamlike intrinsic presence, not as a target highlight or realistic illumination claim.

## 7. Emotional use of language

Prefer:

- “This selection is not credited because…”
- “The report shows…”
- “One adjustment is…”
- “The model withholds…”
- “Retry the same scenario”

Avoid:

- “You failed to understand…”
- “Obvious mistake”
- “Guaranteed victory”
- “Realistic probability”
- “You must save your progress”
- “Don’t miss out”

## 8. Outcome design

Victory and loss use the same architecture:

1. outcome title and score;
2. explicit threshold comparison;
3. component breakdown;
4. evidence-linked findings;
5. one feasible adjustment per finding;
6. complete timeline;
7. undo, retry, return, and new-scenario options.

The goal is not to remove disappointment. It is to keep disappointment attributable, bounded, and actionable.

## 9. Evaluation questions

- Do players describe choices in terms of mission and conditions rather than score alone?
- Does the environment increase attention without obscuring the current task?
- Can players distinguish uncertainty from randomness and concealment from missing information?
- Does a loss produce a testable adjustment rather than helplessness?
- Is the privacy choice remembered accurately after play begins?
- Do experienced players feel respected without first-time players feeling abandoned?
- Does the world feel alive in reduced motion through composition, depth, and color rather than animation alone?
