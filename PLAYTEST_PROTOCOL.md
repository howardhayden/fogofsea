# FOG OF SEA — Comprehension Playtest Protocol

This protocol measures whether players can explain why a force fits a problem, rather than merely learning which choices raise a score. It is an evaluation instrument, not a claim that participant sessions have already occurred.

## Participant and session plan

- Recruit at least eight participants, including first-time players of strategy games and experienced players of strategy games.
- Use session-only mode unless a participant explicitly chooses device-local saving.
- Assign each participant two contrasting operating profiles. Counterbalance profile order across participants.
- Ask participants to think aloud, but do not explain the scoring model or identify a correct force.
- Retain only volunteered, non-identifying notes. Do not record accounts, contact details, device identifiers, or background telemetry.

## Tasks

1. Identify the warfare areas and state the desired end condition.
2. Select two theories and explain whether the second complements or challenges the first.
3. Design a legal force within the point limit.
4. Before seeing the readiness review, explain which environmental and mission constraints drove the force composition.
5. Predict one substitution that should remain viable and one that should create a meaningful gap.
6. Explain why the same force should or should not fit the contrasting profile.
7. Complete the command phase and use the debrief to name one cause, one piece of turn evidence, and one adjustment.
8. At a narrow viewport, open the workspace menu, enter Visualization, switch one plot layer, and return to the current decision without reloading.

## Measures

Score the participant's unaided explanation before revealing the readiness review.

| Measure | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mission fit | Cannot name a mission requirement | Names a requirement without linking a capability | Links one requirement to a capability | Explains several linked requirements and tradeoffs |
| Environment fit | Ignores the operating profile | Repeats a label | Connects one condition to one choice | Explains why the mix changes across profiles |
| Compatibility | Does not recognize hosting or capacity | Notices a warning after prompting | Explains one host relationship | Anticipates hosting, capacity, and mission-credit consequences |
| Strategic logic | Chooses by apparent score only | Names an objective or theory | Connects a theory to an end condition | Compares two mechanisms and a controlling guardrail |
| Debrief learning | Cannot state a cause | Repeats the outcome title | Uses one report as evidence | Connects cause, evidence, and a feasible adjustment |

Also record:

- Time to first legal force.
- Number of blocked or reversed selections.
- Whether the participant noticed the ordered next-step disclosure without prompting.
- Accuracy of the cross-profile prediction.
- Whether the participant changed a force because of reasoning, a visible score, or both.
- Whether compact Visualization opened on the first activation, the menu disappeared, and the participant could return without losing state.
- A one-to-five confidence rating before and after the debrief.

## Release thresholds

- At least 75% of participants score 2 or higher on mission fit, environment fit, and compatibility without moderator explanation.
- At least 70% correctly predict whether their force transfers to the contrasting profile.
- At least 75% can state a cause, cite turn evidence, and propose an adjustment after the debrief.
- Fewer than 20% describe the score as their only reason for a force choice.
- No participant is trapped by the ordered disclosure, keyboard path, save controls, or debrief navigation.

If a threshold is missed, classify the problem as wording, information order, rule transparency, or model tuning; revise that layer; and repeat the affected task with new participants. Do not reinterpret a missed threshold as a pass.

## Moderator record

For each participant, record an anonymous session code, assigned profile order, the five rubric scores, timing, cross-profile prediction, score-versus-reasoning response, observed navigation barriers, and concise quotes paraphrased rather than transcribed. Aggregate results in a separate local document; do not add participant data to the game or its release archive.
